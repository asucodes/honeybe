Method and research

Most bug bounty lists rank by reward size and freshness. honeybe ranks by whether a program actually pays, how crowded it is, and whether its scope fits the skills you really have. Every step below is arithmetic, published, and reproducible from the JSON this site serves.

How the data moves

Why payout reliability, not reward size

The largest prize on a page is not the prize most hunters can win. In a survey of 159 bug bounty hunters, saturation ranked 10th and program age 13th among the factors they actually weigh, while poor responsiveness and payout disputes were the two biggest problems they reported (Akgul et al., arXiv:2301.04781). So the score leads with whether a program responds and pays, then how crowded it is, and only then how large its rewards are.

1. What is scored

Only HackerOne programs that offer bounties and are open to submissions. At the last crawl that is 221 programs. Every other program, including unpaid HackerOne VDPs, Bugcrowd, Intigriti, YesWeHack and direct programs, appears in the Catalog and is never scored.

3. The formula

novelty  = (age_months + 1) ^ -0.40
crowding  = (reports_90d / 3) / max(scope_count, 1)
inverse_crowd  = 1 / (1 + crowding ^ 1.2)
payout_reliability = 0.5 * (efficiency / 100)
  + 0.3 * (paid_90d > 0)
  + 0.2 * exp(-days_since_resolved / 90)
value_per_comp  = min(1, log1p(avg_bounty) / log1p(20000)) / (1 + crowding)
triage_quality  = (efficiency / 100) * {1.0 ok, 0.75 missed, 0.5 failed}
scope_breadth  = min(1, log1p(scope_count) / log1p(500))

base  = 100 * (0.22 novelty + 0.22 inverse_crowd + 0.22 payout_reliability
  + 0.16 value_per_comp + 0.11 triage_quality + 0.07 scope_breadth)
score = clamp(base + penalties, 0, 100)

6. Personalisation, and why it cannot lie

Your profile changes the order, never the facts. Four rules keep it honest, and public/engine.test.js asserts each one.

Rule Effect
Experience level scales every self-rating: beginner x0.5, intermediate x0.85, advanced x1.0
Profile strength personal weight = 15% x completeness, so a half-filled profile applies half-strength
Bounded influence a personal score moves at most plus or minus 12 points from the population score
Skill gap simulates learning a weak surface to 3/5 and counts the programs that would enter your top 50
Worked example. A program scores 68.6 for everyone. If your profile is 80% complete and your effective stack matches, your personal score can land anywhere between 56.6 and 80.6. The delta is printed in every expanded card, together with the cap. A profile can reorder the list; it cannot turn a 40 into a 90.

Surface mismatch is share-weighted over the two largest surfaces: an effective rating at or below 1 costs 18 points times its share, a rating of 2 costs 8. Tools amplify a surface you already practice and can never create skill from zero. Short sessions discount very large scopes.

7. The Catalog: breadth with honest gaps

The Catalog is the honest answer to a simple question: what else is out there, and what do we actually know about it? It merges four open sources: the arkadiyt mirrors for HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti and YesWeHack, plus the lissy93 lists for platform and direct programs. Rows are deduplicated on platform:slug and merged, so a program listed twice keeps the better field from each source.

Loading catalog counts.

Figure 4. Catalog rows by source and the fields each row can prove. 

points  = 45 * bounty_known
        + 30 * min(1, log1p(scope_count) / log1p(400))
        + 15 * min(1, scope_types / 6)
        + 10 * disclosure_terms
        + 10 * min(1, log1p(max_payout) / log1p(1000000))

order   = 100 * points / sum(weights of known fields)

Points are normalised over the fields that exist, so a missing payout figure neither helps nor hurts. Every row prints a coverage percentage over four checks: bounty status, payout, scope and disclosure terms. The Catalog also tracks when honeybe first saw each program, which is what powers the "Latest additions" ordering. That date means first seen by us, not the program's launch date, and it is labelled that way.

What the Catalog is not. It is not a payout prediction. Catalog rows carry no report counts, no response efficiency and no payout history, so a high catalog order is only a statement that the published fields look broad and open. Missing data is printed as missing, never as a good score.

8. How to verify us

  1. Download programs.json. Every signal, weight, penalty and component contribution is in the file.
  2. Recompute any row by hand with the formula above. The components sum to the score before penalties, and penalties are listed per row.
  3. Run the personalisation tests: node public/engine.test.js. They assert bounds, monotonicity, cold start and the beginner guard.
  4. Read the scoring code: pipeline/score.py and pipeline/badges.py. There is no model, no ranking service and no hidden tiebreaker.
  5. Check the network panel. The page loads static files and makes no calls with your profile. Personalisation runs locally and is stored in localStorage.

9. Research

  2. Akgul et al. Bug hunters' perspectives on bug bounty programs. arXiv:2301.04781. 159 hunters. Source of the payout reliability and crowding priorities.
  3. Zheng et al. An analysis of 9,942 HackerOne reports. arXiv:2511.18608. Source of the decision not to model reporter reputation.

10. Limits

  • Signals come from unauthenticated HackerOne endpoints. Anything behind a login is absent.
  • Catalog rows carry no report counts. That is why they are not scored.
  • Slop risk is a single-snapshot proxy and is labeled experimental.
  • Scope data is refreshed from community mirrors every 30 minutes upstream, and merged here every 6 hours.
  • First-seen dates start at the first crawl. Until two crawls exist, "Latest additions" means "everything", and the UI says so.
